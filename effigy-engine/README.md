# Effigy Engine

A procedural 3D character and monster designer. Shape a grim bust with sliders, paint it, then export a printable STL or a round PNG token for a virtual tabletop.

The sculpt is the form that prints; the paint is only for the picture. **Paint** (`src/paint.ts`, `src/paintPanel.ts`) colours the skin, eyes (optionally glowing), hair, beard and brows, horns, and teeth and tusks, with a Grime slider; each race starts with its own colours. The skin is a shader: pores, furrows and age creases as a per-pixel height field, and per-vertex weights worked out for each bust (shading in the hollows, flushed cheeks, the red of the lips, age, stubble, the scalp under the hair, grime). The hair is darker down its carved grooves. A change of colour only changes the materials' settings and never rebuilds the bust, and none of it reaches the STL or the print preview, which stay clay. **Clay** in the toolbar shows the bare form. **Save token PNG** (`src/token.ts`) saves the painted head and shoulders, turned a little toward three-quarter view, in a round frame with a dark iron rim, transparent outside it, at 256, 512 or 1024 px. Framing "Whole head and horns" fits everything above the chin (horns, hair, ears) inside the round as it is seen in that view; "Close portrait" frames the face larger and may crop wide horns. The picture and its file name are of the character as it was when the button was pressed. **Backdrop** stands the bust on one of the pictures in `public/backdrops/` (a dungeon, a forest, a forge and others, original images made for the project: see `CREDITS.md` there) instead of the plain dark glow, the whole picture drawn just as wide as the round inside the rim. The shoulders and chest always run on under the lower half of the rim, so no cut edge of the bust shows (found by drawing the bust's silhouette small, off screen, and bringing it down or nearer until its edge is covered); on Whole head and horns, very wide horns come first, so a bust with them may stay smaller. A **preview** at the top of the Token section shows the token as it would be saved, redrawn as the backdrop or framing changes and a moment after the character or its paint stops changing; ‹ and › beside Backdrop step through the backdrops to try each in turn. Archetype presets from the first version are in git history (up to commit `ca8ec31`); clothing and armour are still to come.

Stack: TypeScript, Vite, Three.js, [manifold-3d](https://github.com/elalish/manifold) (merging parts into one solid), Vitest, and [Playwright](https://playwright.dev) for the browser tests in `e2e/` (the token picture: its sizes and transparency, horns inside the rim, and the view left as it was). They start their own dev server on port 5203 and render WebGL in software, so they take a couple of minutes. Backdrop images for the token are in `public/backdrops/`, listed in its `backdrops.json` (see the note there).

```
npm install
npm run dev     # http://localhost:5201
npm test
npm run test:browser   # Playwright, in Chromium (first: npx playwright install chromium)
npm run build
```

The humanoid sculpt uses the shared forms in `src/sculptForms.ts`: defined human cheek/jaw planes, a fuller swept hairstyle, shaped hairlines and beard contours across the eight humanoid races. Long hair has a broader layered back curtain and two substantial side tresses of different lengths. These are mesh changes used by both the viewport and STL builder. The hair's thin edge takes the same root tone as the scalp just past it, so the hairline has no hard line (the human's sculpted planes and finer brows are race data: `planes`, `browHeight`, `browCarve` in `src/params.ts`). `src/shellRegion.ts` removes pinched growth boundaries before a hair/beard shell is extruded; carving stays inside the authored shell envelope. Dragonborn continues through its separate sculpt and builder, with its existing controls and assets.

The panel has three tabs: **Shape** (race, build, styles and the sliders), **Appearance** (the paint) and **Output** (the token and the print); the token preview is drawn only while Output is open. Each slider section and the paint have a Reset back to the race's values. **Undo** and **Redo** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y) step through the sculpt and its paint together, a slider drag counting as one step. **Save** writes the character (its sculpt and paint) to a small JSON file, versioned, which **Open** reads back to go on editing it (see src/character.ts).

Each build and token is timed in the browser as a User Timing measure (`effigy:draft build`, `effigy:carved build`, `effigy:edit to draft`, `effigy:token fit`, `effigy:token`): see the Performance panel, or `performance.getEntriesByType("measure")` in the console. A slider dragged during a build is built once for where it got to, not for every step; the view is drawn only when something in it changes; and the token's camera is fitted once per bust and framing, so stepping through backdrops fits nothing again.

## How the bust is made

The head, neck and shoulders are a **hand-sculpted CC0 base mesh** (Blender Studio's Human Base Meshes, see `public/models/README.md`), subdivided once to about 53,000 vertices so it can carry sharp structure, so the lips, nostrils, eyelids, ears and collarbones are real anatomy. Every slider, race and build is a small, smooth **deformation of that mesh**: push the brow outward, hollow the cheek, lengthen the nose tip, scale the lips about the mouth line, widen the jaw, stretch the ears. Nothing moves right around the eyes, so the lids always fit the eyeballs. The mesh is a closed solid, so it is already printable.

The **Dragonborn** is not pushed out of the human head (it was tried, and looked wrong): it is a family of its own, chosen at the end of the Race list, with a sculpt of its own (`src/dragonborn.ts`). That sculpt is one fixed, closed-mouthed bust built by `tools/sculpt_dragonborn.py` in Blender (see `assets/dragonborn/README.md`). It is loaded only the first time it is chosen (about 9 MB), and if it will not load the bust goes back to the humanoid and the panel says why. The humanoid's build, styles and face sliders are hidden while it is shown and are kept as they were for when a race is chosen again; it has its own instead, under **Dragonborn** (muzzle length and width, jaw breadth, brow plates, neck girth) and **Horns and spines** (horn size and sweep, spine size), and shares **Torso size** and the **Expression** sliders: it has a real mouth behind its lips (a slit along the lip line, through to a hollow with two rows of teeth and a tongue, and two fangs that show over the lower lip even shut), and Jaw open turns its lower jaw on a hinge, up to 16 degrees, gliding a little forward as it opens, by weights smooth over the space they cross (not over the surface, which dips into every groove between the scales: those folded) so the corners and throat follow; Snarl lifts the upper lip and bares the teeth, Scowl brings the brow plates down, Smile draws the corners of the mouth up and back. Each is a smooth regional warp of the sculpt, as the humanoid's sliders are of the human head: the scales were cut into the sculpt, so they move and stretch a little with its surface; the eyes never move; each horn and spine moves with the skin it grows from and is resized from its root, from a little past where it leaves the skin. Every control is tested at both ends for a surface that folds over itself (no triangle anywhere may turn over), the jaw at every tenth of its opening with the neck, torso, snarl and smile at their extremes, and for a print that stays one solid. Its paint uses the same pickers (the Skin row reads Scales: with the bronze, brass and copper of the metallic dragons), eyes and horns, but its own materials: each scale its own slight shade, from the same cells the sculpt cut it from, darker down the grooves between them, the throat's scutes and the underside of the jaw paler and warmer, as a reptile's belly; and eyes all iris, with a vertical slit pupil, that glow if asked. Its print and token go through the same stand, fit, merge and framing as any bust. At tabletop size it comes out about 32 mm tall (its torso slimmed to about 80%): centred by its whole outline (see below) it fills the square from the muzzle's tip to the back of its horns, and that depth is what sets its size (a shorter muzzle or horns swept less far back let it grow).

Eyes, horns, tusks and hair are separate meshes. The eyeballs are fitted to their sockets so the lids rest on them; horns and tusks are sculpted assets (`tools/export_horns.py`) seated on the surface by ray casting; hair is a closed shell raised from the scalp (`src/hairShell.ts`; long hair adds a draped curtain for its hanging lengths). Beards are sculpted the same way over the lower face (`src/beard.ts`). Tusks belong to half-orcs only.

**Clothing** (the Outfit and Cloak lists, under the race; `src/garments.ts`): none, a tunic, leather (a sleeveless jerkin over the tunic, with a V at the front, a raised seam inside its edge and a baldric over the right shoulder, and a leather spaulder over each shoulder, a low dome in three overlapping bands, covering its arm holes as the pauldrons cover the breastplate's), mail (a mail shirt over the tunic, from just below its collar over the shoulders and down, with a binding round its neck and modelled rings all over it: rows of overlapping rings, each row leaning the other way, about 2 mm apart and 0.7 mm proud at full size; the body's triangles are too large for them, so the mail's outside alone is divided into sixteen for each, and it is the heaviest outfit to build and draw), or armour (a breastplate, a gorget in three lames and two pauldrons over the tunic), on either family; and over any of them, or nothing, a cloak, plain or with a cowl (its hood thrown back and gathered round the back of the neck), with a domed metal clasp each side of its open front. Each piece is fitted to the bare body (`src/bodySurface.ts`), never to the scene: the body's own triangles inside a line drawn on it (a low neckline, the edge of the plate with an opening for each arm, a round over each shoulder), cut exactly along it, stood off what is under it by a thickness, and closed into a solid that prints joined to the bust. Its outside is stretched over the body rather than painted on it: it spans the hollows and keeps its clearance from the body smoothed (a tunic over the broad forms, plate over none but the broadest), so a Dragonborn's scales do not show through, and it is checked against the body itself and pushed off wherever it would touch. Then its relief: a rolled collar and soft folds on the tunic, a ridge down the breastplate and a rolled edge round it, the pauldrons stepped into three overlapping plates. (The relief is evened out over a few of the body's points, and each edge smoothed along itself on both its sides, so the edge of a plate runs clean rather than scalloped; the wall round it is shaded apart from its faces, and welded to them again for the print. The gorget is grown over the breastplate itself, so it rises over the ridge rather than coming down onto it, and the breastplate's ridge and rolled edge begin below the gorget. Relief is raised along the body's evened-out normals, not the outside's own, which turned every way along a crinkled edge; the cloak, which hangs away from the body, along its own.) The cloak goes on last, over all of that: it keeps its clearance from what is worn as well as from that smoothed, so the edges of the pauldrons do not show through it (it rises over them in a soft swell), and below the widest it comes out from the body it hangs straight down rather than following the body back in, with folds down the back. Its clasps are small turned solids set into it, as the body's triangles are too large to draw them. The tunic thins to nothing at the flat cut, so the pedestal still meets the body with no ledge to print over. The hair and the beard are built over the clothes (long hair hangs over the armour, and a full beard lowers the collar at the front). A Dragonborn's clothes are fitted to a coarser copy of its skin written by the sculpt script (its skin is some 330,000 triangles; a tunic fitted to it was 300,000, and the print's merge ran out of memory). A fit takes a second or two (a Dragonborn's longer), so on the page it is made in a worker (`src/fitWorker.ts`, `src/fitClient.ts`): the bust on show stays until the new one is built, and only the latest settings asked for meanwhile are fitted. Each fit is kept by the shape of the body wherever a garment can reach (with a margin) and the anchors it is placed by, not by the settings, so an edit of the face, the brows, the nose or the expression puts the same outfit on again at once, while the weight, the muscle, the torso, the jaw and the neck fit it anew. Old character files wear nothing; the outfit is part of what prints (the print key), its colours (Cloth, Leather, Armour, Cloak) are paint and never rebuild it. Tested: both families print as one closed solid in every outfit, with and without a cloak, and in armour and a cowl at both ends of Torso size; every garment stands off the body above the cut; and nothing worn shows through the cloak but at its very edge (under 0.6% of the points under it: the top of the gorget above its collar at the back of the neck, and the tops of the pauldrons at its edge; a sampled check, not a proof of none anywhere). At tabletop size the clothes' relief, sized for the full-size print, shrinks with the bust, and the print preview says so where its finest relief comes out under the 0.6 mm that reliably prints (in armour and a cowl, the 0.5 mm rims of the clasps stand about 0.2 mm); the shapes still print. No test coupon has been printed yet. Clothing widens the bust at the shoulders, above where Torso size slims it, so at tabletop size it costs height: a human 46 mm bare, 43 mm in a tunic, 42 in leather, 33 in armour, 42 in a cloak alone, 37 in leather and a cowl, 31 in armour and a cowl; a Dragonborn 32, 31, 30, 27, 31, 28 and 25 mm. Known rough edges: the Dragonborn's breastplate and jerkin are uneven at the back corner of each arm, and its cloak over armour is faintly pitted down the back.

Print target: an FDM printer with a 0.2 mm nozzle (one profile, in `src/print.ts`), busts 75-90 mm tall on a turned pedestal 34 mm tall: a round foot (at least 50 mm across, wider only when a broad bust needs it) with a chamfered band and ledge, a second narrower stepped tier, a cove sweeping in to a shaft with a doubled collar at its foot, a ring where the flare begins, and a flare out to the bust's exact underside, which it meets flush. Every part of it either narrows going up or widens no faster than 45 degrees (the collars are diamonds in section, not round beads with undercuts, jutting 0.55-0.7 mm), so it prints without supports. The mesh tucks in under the shoulders, so its underside is not flat: the bust is sliced at fine heights and cut flat at the lowest plane above which the outline never grows outward faster than 45 degrees (2.5 to 4 mm up, depending on the build), so nothing hangs out over the cut. The pedestal's flare matches the section at that cut exactly and is joined to it. The flare starts as low as the bust's reach needs, so it is no steeper than about 45 degrees from vertical and prints without supports (measured on the exported files: 45.2 degrees at most). Its top stays 0.05 mm inside the section, so it meets the bust flush. Surface texture has to be real geometry at that scale (grooves at least about 0.4 mm wide, ridges at least 0.6 mm), so hair and beard detail is sculpted, not shaded. The export checks the file as a slicer reads it (every edge shared by exactly two triangles) and refuses otherwise; `python tools/check-stl.py <file.stl>` re-checks any exported file independently with Trimesh. Every export also measures its wall thickness (`src/thickness.ts`: rays along locally averaged normals, so carved texture on a thick core does not read as thin) and reports the share of the surface under 0.4 and 0.6 mm, and how many rays found no far side (left out of both sides: the shares are of the walls measured). (Until 1 Nov 2026 the sampler skipped some of the surface it meant to measure: the earlier figure of 0.1-2% was measured with it and should be taken as provisional.) These sizes are design targets: print a small detail coupon at your actual settings before relying on them.

Tabletop size (the "Tabletop size" checkbox on the export, on by default; the file name gets -tabletop): the whole bust, hair, beard, horns and shoulders included, inside a one inch (25.4 mm) square of a gaming board, on the same turned pedestal, 25 mm across and 18 mm tall (a board has no ceiling, so height is free), its moldings sized in printed millimetres so they read on a 0.4 mm nozzle at any scale, as near 50 mm tall as that allows (`src/fit.ts`). A bust is far wider and deeper than it is tall, so scaling it alone cannot give both. The sculpt is scaled as large as the square allows (never past the height aimed for), and the body is slimmed with the Torso size slider only as far as that scale needs. The bust stands over the base by the middle of its whole outline seen from above, not of its underside, since the square is centred on the base: a head that reaches far forward or back no longer runs past one side while the other has room (the pedestal meets the underside from where it then is; the shift is held to half the underside's reach, so it stays under the chest). It made the Dragonborn a quarter larger (23 to 29 mm; with its muzzle then sculpted 1.5 cm shorter, 32 mm); the humanoids, whose width sets their size, are as they were. The head and its hair do not slim, so on the busts tried it is their depth, nose or beard to the back of the hair, that sets the size: busts come out 40-44 mm tall, under the 45 mm wanted, with the torso slimmed to about 60-70% (never below 60%: thinner, the body deforms too far), and the export says so. The fit is measured on quick drafts and checked on the parts that print (`src/bounds.ts`: including where the merge trims a part at the flat cut, which can reach farther out than any of its vertices above it), and refuses a file that does not fit, checking the merged file once more before it is saved. Everything the printer must hold (grooves, ridges, lock thickness) shrinks with the scale, so at tabletop size the 0.4 and 0.6 mm targets above are about half that: the carving needs a 0.2 mm nozzle and is not yet re-sized for the small print. **Preview print** (in the Print section) shows the print before it is saved, in place of the sculpt: the very parts the export will merge (slimmed, scaled and on the small base at tabletop size, with the gusset and the slightly larger eyes), cut where the merge cuts them, over the one inch square, with its size; Export STL then saves those parts. Any edit to the sculpt goes back to it.

## Layout

- `public/models/head.bin` and `feminine.bin` - the base head (57,799 vertices) with a masculine and a feminine chest sculpted from the CC0 bodies, with source and license in the README beside them
- `tools/export_head.py` - the Blender script that produced `head.bin` from the CC0 bundle (including the shoulders and chest, wrapped onto the bundle's sculpted male and female bodies)
- `tools/export_horns.py` - generates the horn and tusk assets in `public/models/horns/`
- `public/models/dragonborn.bin`, `dragonborn.json`, `src/dragonborn.ts` - the Dragonborn's own model and how it is loaded and built (made by `tools/sculpt_dragonborn.py --app public/models`)
- `src/assets.ts` - loads the head, horns and tusk
- `src/hair.ts` - where on the scalp hair grows
- `src/headMesh.ts` - loads the head, moves it to the eyes' midpoint, computes normals and connectivity, and holds the measured landmarks
- `src/params.ts` - the character as one plain object: sliders, races (each with a skeleton), styles, and `derive`, which turns sliders into anatomy
- `src/deform.ts` - every slider as a regional displacement of the base head
- `src/face.ts` - the deformed bust mesh, and surface lookups by ray casting
- `src/builder.ts` - assembles the head, eyes, tusks, horns, hair and beard into a Three.js group
- `src/hairShell.ts`, `src/beard.ts` - hair and beard shells, and where a beard grows
- `src/brows.ts` - sculpted eyebrows: the brow outline, raised and carved with grooves along the hair
- `src/printable.ts`, `src/export.ts`, `src/exportWorker.ts`, `src/exportStl.ts` - the STL export: every part of the bust is baked into millimetres (Z up, mirrored parts re-wound), welded, split into its closed shells, and merged with manifold-3d in a worker into one solid; a binary STL and a report (pieces, voids, size, volume) come out. The **Export STL** button is in the panel.
- `src/jaw.ts` - opening the mouth: the lower jaw turns about its hinge, with per-vertex weights solved on the mesh itself (the head has a real hollow mouth behind the lips, so opening parts them and the surface stays closed)
- `src/tusk.ts` - a half-orc's tusk: a tooth of the lower jaw in place of the canine, swept along a curve from its root in the gum forward through the parting of the lips (which part round it) and up in front of the upper lip
- `src/teeth.ts` - an upper and a lower row of teeth with their gums, and a tongue, in the mouth's cavity (shown only when the mouth is parted); the lower row and the tongue turn with the jaw
- `src/mouthFill.ts` - the hollow behind the lips (a pocket about 13 x 16 x 9 mm whose roof is a flat ceiling over air) is filled solid: the convex hull of the pocket's own walls, grown a few percent so it joins the head. It sits inside the head and cannot be seen; it takes the mouth roof out of the overhangs and keeps supports out of a pocket nobody could reach. With the jaw open it is filled too, all but a recess 3 mm behind the front teeth, so the mouth still reads as open (a print with the mouth open, 2 Nov 2026, showed the whole pocket's roof printing as a 15 mm bridge that sagged into a hole)
- `src/gusset.ts`, `src/hull.ts` - an option on the export (the "Throat gusset" checkbox, on by default; the file name gets -nogusset when it is off): a wedge under the chin that slopes back to the neck at 45 degrees (found by casting rays through a bounding volume hierarchy, which took it from about 17 s to under 1 s), so the chin's flat underside (about 9 mm of overhang, 50-130 mm2 at 70 degrees) needs no supports. It adds 0.3-0.7 cm3 and makes the throat heavier; with it off, print with the slicer's supports under the chin instead (tree supports, overhang 55 degrees). Measured on exports: steep overhang (over 55 degrees) per bust falls from 106-225 mm2 to 54-122 mm2
- `src/locks.ts` - dreadlocks: each lock a closed tube that falls under gravity and goes on falling until its tip rests on the head, neck or shoulders (a printer lays the tip first, so a tip over open air has nothing to start on), with spiral grooves, over a close crop
- `src/carve.ts` - subdivides a hair or beard shell and cuts a groove pattern into its outer surface
- `src/beardCarve.ts`, `src/hairCarve.ts` - the patterns: broad locks with a finer groove down each, running the way the hair is combed (a moustache fan for beards; radiating from the crown for a crop; down the curtain and locks of long hair), sized for the printer
- `src/print.ts` - the print scale (mm per head unit), the smallest groove and feature that print, and the tabletop size
- `src/fit.ts` - tabletop size: scales the bust and slims its torso to fit a one inch square (see above)
- `src/printPreview.ts` - the print preview: the parts as the export will merge them, shown in place of the sculpt
- `src/fitWorker.ts`, `src/fitClient.ts` - clothing fitted off the page: an edit that changes the body under the clothes has a worker fit them, while the bust on show stays
- `src/prepareWorker.ts`, `src/prepareClient.ts` - the print prepared off the page: Preview print and Export STL (when nothing matching was previewed) have a worker collect the parts and run the tabletop fit, whose builds take seconds, while the view stays live
- `src/parts.ts` - what each named part of a bust is, in one table: the material it is painted with, what the fit's notes call it, its role (body, feature, outgrowth, hair, support, base) and how the print treats it (eyes grown 7%); paint, fit and export read it
- `src/bodySurface.ts` - the bare body that clothing and armour will be fitted to, for either family: one frame, a cached nearest-point search, the flat cut, and anchors found from the surface (the neck's base, sternum, nape, the tops of the shoulders, a ring round the neck)
- `src/ui.ts` - the control panel
- `src/main.ts` - scene, camera, lights, drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, double-click to face front
