Side Quest (Effigy Engine):

Paint and tokens. The sculpt is what prints, but for a virtual tabletop people want a picture, so the bust can now be painted and saved as a round PNG token. The skin is a shader with pores, furrows and age creases, shading in the hollows, flushed cheeks, lips, stubble and a Grime slider. The eyes can glow. The hair is darker down its carved grooves. None of it reaches the STL, which stays plain clay, and a Clay button shows the bare form. Save token PNG puts the painted head and shoulders in a round iron frame, transparent outside it, at 256, 512 or 1024 px.

Codex made ten original backdrop pictures for the tokens (a dungeon, a misty forest, a forge, a graveyard and others). The Token section has a live preview of the token as it would be saved, with arrows to step through the backdrops. The shoulders always run on under the rim, so no cut edge of the bust shows. The framing fits wide horns inside the round as they are seen in the token's turned view. I added Playwright browser tests for all of this: the saved file is square, the right size, transparent outside the rim, and nothing on "Whole head and horns" pokes past the rim.

Painting showed problems that the clay had hidden. A thin film of hair lay over the tops of the ears, beards wrapped round behind the ears and over the earlobe, and with a snarl the mouth's filling poked through the upper lip as two dark dots under the nose. Each was fixed with a test across five or six races: the hairline tucks round and behind the ear, the beard's sideburn stops at the front of the ear, and the filling is checked to stay inside the face at every age and expression. The torso slider now stops at 60%, because below that the body deformed too far.

Audit 18 rounded out the day. The panel was split into Shape, Appearance and Output tabs, with undo and redo, a reset for each section, and character files you can save and load. The hairline got softer, the locks uneven widths, and the skin grain and creases gentler.
