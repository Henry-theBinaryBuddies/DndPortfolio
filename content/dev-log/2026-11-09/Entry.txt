Side Quest (Scenic Sorcerer):

I started a third side quest: a map maker for tabletop games, in the spirit of Inkarnate, with square and hex grids, brushes, land tools, stamps, shapes and text. Before any code, Claude searched for what players complain about in the tools they use. The same few things came up: exports that are only a flat picture, so walls and doors have to be redrawn in the tabletop; hex grids that do not line up when the map is imported; maps locked to the size they were started at; paywalls and price rises; AI art (Inkarnate was boycotted over it); and editors that need an internet connection. These came from forum posts and review round-ups, so I treated them as a direction, not as proof.

Then Claude asked questions until the design was settled. I chose battle maps first, with regional, town and hex-crawl maps and generators later; drawing as vector data, so a map has no fixed size; maps kept in the browser and in a saved file; free, no account and no AI; and the name Scenic Sorcerer. I dropped Fantasy Foundry because it sounds like Foundry VTT, a tool the maps export to. I also asked for a very clean, simple screen that anyone could use. Claude proposed seven steps, each ending with the tests passing and something I could open, and I agreed.

Steps 1 to 4 were built today. 1: a blank map with square, flat-top and pointy-top hex grids, panning, zooming, snapping, unlimited undo, autosave, and saving and opening a file. 2: rooms, walls, doors and windows that snap onto walls and move with their room, and floor textures painted by code. I then asked for a way to switch the walls off for people who do not want the black outline, which became a per-room setting. 3: a texture brush, stamps from pictures you add yourself, a stamp library kept in the browser, and layers. 4: text with preset looks, and a GM layer with room numbers that count up by themselves, traps, secret doors and notes.

Testing in a browser found one real bug. Closing the tab within half a second of a change lost the change, because autosave waits that long. It now saves as the tab closes.
