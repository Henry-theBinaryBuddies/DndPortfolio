Artifact 4 (CouchCrew):

The video call worked for all three of us in a test today: John, Adrian and I in one call at once, each on a different network, with a YouTube video playing above it and chat open (photo added to the card). Adrian has no webcam, so he joined as a viewer and shows as a letter, while John and I were on camera.

A correction to my own story. After my laptop and phone failed to see each other on different networks, I added a relay (our server asks Cloudflare's TURN service for short-lived credentials when you join a call, with the secret key kept on the server). I assumed the cause was two devices on different networks being unable to connect directly. It was actually a firewall on my work network blocking video calls. So the relay was built for the wrong diagnosis. It is merged and harmless, but when I checked the live server today it reported the relay as off (its key was never added on Render), and I have not shown it fixing anything. Today's three-network call suggests direct connections are enough for most of our networks, since the relay appears to have been off when it worked. A relay is still the safety net for a network that blocks direct connections.

Two other things cleared up. The YouTube "sign in to confirm you're not a bot" message was a VPN on one of our connections; turning it off fixed it, so it was never a bug in our code. And I worked out that a 10-person room would use the relay's free 1,000 GB in around 25 hours if everything went through it, and would need about 9 megabits per second of upload from each person, which is why the team capped rooms at 5 people.

Since then I built the extras I had listed: switching mic, camera and speaker during a call without leaving it, a loudness meter on every tile with a ring around whoever is speaking, a mute button and volume slider for each other person (this only changes what you hear), a first-visit tips panel in the room, and a leader-set room name that leaves the link alone. I tested all of it with two browser windows and a fake camera and microphone and pushed it for a teammate's review (the call controls were merged on Nov 8; the tips and room name are still in review). Not tested: those extras on a phone.

What is left is cosmetic, and John is taking the visual polish.

Side Quest (Effigy Engine):

Clothing. An Outfit list (tunic or armour) and a Cloak list, fitted to the body of either family. Each garment is grown from the body: the body's triangles inside a line drawn on it, cut exactly along that line, pushed out to a smoothed clearance (so a Dragonborn's scales don't show through), given relief (a rolled collar and folds, a ridged breastplate, stepped pauldrons) and closed into a solid that prints joined to the bust. Hair and beards are built over the clothes, so a full beard lowers the collar.

Later the same day: a leather jerkin over the tunic, with a V neck, a raised seam and a baldric; a gorget of three plates with the armour; and a cloak, plain or with a cowl, with two turned metal clasps, over any outfit. The cloak hangs straight down below its widest point and rises softly over the pauldrons. Every outfit, with and without a cloak, prints as one closed solid on both families.

The cost is size at tabletop: clothing widens the shoulders, and the width sets the size. A human is 46 mm tall bare, 43 mm in a tunic and 33 mm in armour; a Dragonborn 32, 31 and 27 mm.

Audit 21 in the evening: fitting clothes took seconds, so it moved to a worker too. A fit is now kept until the body's shape changes, so an edit to the face puts the same outfit back on at once. Plate edges were smoothed, and the gorget now grows over the breastplate so it clears the ridge instead of notching into it.
