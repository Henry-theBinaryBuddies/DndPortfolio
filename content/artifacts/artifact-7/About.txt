Side Quest

Effigy Engine

A designer for 3D busts of fantasy characters, built to end up as a miniature on the table or a token on a virtual one. Pick a race (human, elf, half-elf, dwarf, halfling, gnome, half-orc, tiefling or Dragonborn) and shape the face with sliders for age, weight, muscle, brow, cheekbones, nose, lips, jaw and heavy lids. Add hair (cropped, swept, mohawk, long or dreadlocks), a beard, horns or tusks, and open the jaw for teeth, a tongue and an expression. Dress it in a tunic, a leather jerkin with spaulders, a mail shirt or plate armour, with a cloak or a cowl over any of them. It is live at effigy.creaturecauldron.com.

The humanoid faces start from a hand-sculpted base head (a free, public-domain base mesh from Blender Studio) that the code deforms for each race and slider, so they come from an artist's sculpt and not from primitives. The Dragonborn is sculpted separately by a Blender script, with its scales, horns and spines cut in, and has sliders of its own. Hair, beards and eyebrows are carved shells with strands cut into them. Clothing is grown from the body's own surface, so it fits either family at any setting.

Two outputs. Export STL merges everything, base included, into one closed solid for printing, full size (about 120 mm tall) or tabletop size (inside a one inch square). It refuses to save a file that is not exactly one solid, and Preview print shows the bust as it will print first. The pedestal flares no steeper than 45 degrees, the hollow of the mouth is filled, and an optional throat gusset removes the overhang under the chin, so most busts print without supports. Save token PNG paints the bust (skin, eyes, hair, scales, cloth and metal) and saves it in a round iron frame, with a choice of ten original backdrops.

Two test prints of a dwarf showed what reads on PLA: the hair's grooves and the broad forms of the face, but not the finest carving. A third print, to test the clothing, failed at the bed before it proved anything, so the site says plainly that the mail's rings, the cloak and relief under about 1 mm are unproven on a print.

Built with TypeScript, Three.js and Vite, with manifold-3d doing the solid geometry and the heavy fitting in background workers. 185 unit tests and 13 browser tests, plus a timing run against the built site. Hosted as a static site on Cloudflare Pages. The base head and the Dragonborn were made with Blender and Python.
