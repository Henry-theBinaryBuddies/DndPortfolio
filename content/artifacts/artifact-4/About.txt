CouchCrew

A watch-party site made for someone else: Adrian, who watches movies with his long-distance partner, and anyone else who wants to watch together without being in the same room. Make a room, send the link, and everyone who opens it gets text chat, a video call, screen share and a shared YouTube player, all in a phone browser with nothing to install.

We were a team of three. Adrian built the rooms, chat and room-leader roles, John built the synced YouTube playback, and I built the audio, video and screen share.

My part took three tries. A Daily.co call needed a credit card on file, and Jitsi Meet's free public server kept ending calls early and would not let us rejoin. So on Nov 4 Adrian decided to drop third parties and I rebuilt the call as our own peer-to-peer WebRTC: everyone connects directly to everyone else, and our server only passes the setup messages. It has mic, camera, screen share and mute indicators, and people with no camera can still join and watch. I also added a relay server (Cloudflare TURN) after a laptop-and-phone test failed, but the real cause was a firewall on my work network, so the relay is only a safety net and has not been shown working.

Tested: John, Adrian and I joined one room from three different networks and could all talk with a YouTube video playing above the call (the newest photo in the gallery). Adrian has no webcam, so he joined as a letter to watch and listen. Since then I added switching mic, camera and speaker mid-call, loudness meters, and per-person mute and volume; those are merged. First-visit room tips and a leader-set room name are still waiting for review. Still open: a test with a phone on mobile data. John's visual polish pass is now live.

Built with React and Vite on Vercel, and a Node, Express and Socket.IO server on Render's free tier (the first visit after it sleeps can take up to a minute). The plan, reviewed pull requests and a running log are in the repository.

Live: https://couch-crew.vercel.app
