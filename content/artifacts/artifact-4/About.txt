Artifact 4

CouchCrew

A watch-party site made for someone else: Adrian, who watches movies with his long-distance partner, and anyone else who wants to watch something together without being in the same room. Make a room, send the link, and everyone who opens it lands in the same place with text chat, a video call, screen share and a shared YouTube player. It runs in a phone browser, so nobody needs an app or a laptop.

We were a team of three, each with a job and a branch. Adrian built the rooms, presence and chat backbone, then the leader role (one person controls what is playing) and the genre themes. John built the synced YouTube playback and researched whether Netflix and Hulu could be synced too. I built the audio, video and screen share.

My part went through three versions. First I built a Daily.co integration, with a server that made a Daily room for each CouchCrew room and a page with its own tile grid. Adrian tested it live and Daily refused the call, because its free minutes need a card on file. We then embedded Jitsi Meet's free public server, which needs no account, and the three of us had a working call on it. But it kept ending calls early, would not let us rejoin, and since 2023 its public server wants a signed-in moderator, so anonymous rooms were never going to be dependable.

So on Nov 4 Adrian decided to drop third parties and I rebuilt the call as our own peer-to-peer WebRTC. Everyone in a call connects directly to everyone else, and our Socket.IO server only passes the setup messages between them; no video goes through it. It has mic, camera, screen share, a mute and camera-off indicator on each tile, and leaving and rejoining works. Someone with no camera or mic can still join and watch. When two people change something at once, such as both starting to share their screen, a standard "perfect negotiation" pattern keeps the connection from deadlocking. My first test across two networks (laptop on one, phone on another) failed, with each device seeing only itself. I first took that for the usual problem that two devices on different networks cannot connect directly, and added a relay server (our server sets up Cloudflare's TURN service so the secret key never reaches the browser). It turned out the real cause was a firewall on my work network blocking video calls, so the relay was not what that test needed. It is still there as a safety net for networks that block direct connections, but I have not yet shown it working, and when I checked on Nov 6 the live server still reported it as off because its key has not been added on Render. On a phone the screen-share button is hidden because phone browsers generally cannot share a screen, so "one person shares their Netflix tab" is a laptop feature.

Built with React and Vite on Vercel, and a Node, Express and Socket.IO server on Render's free tier (the first visit after it sleeps can take up to a minute). The shared plan, branches, reviewed pull requests and a running log are in the repository.

Tested: the three of us joined one room from our own devices on the peer-to-peer version and could all see each other while a YouTube video played above the call (the newest photo in the gallery). That worked without the relay. Earlier the YouTube player asked us to sign in to confirm we were not bots; that turned out to be a VPN on one of our connections, and turning it off fixed it. After that I added switching mic, camera and speaker during a call, a loudness meter on every tile, per-person mute and volume, first-visit tips, and a leader-set room name; those are pushed and waiting for a teammate's review. Still open: a call with a phone on mobile data from outside any firewall, and a polish pass on the look, which John is taking.
