Artifact 4:
Reflection
I owned the audio, video, and screen share for CouchCrew.
We had to redesign for few reasons, the first being Daily.co hosting
required a credit card on file. The second was that Jitsi's free
server kept ending calls and wouldn't let us rejoin, so I rebuilt the call as our own
peer-to-peer WebRTC. I later added switching mic, camera, and speaker mid-call,
loudness meters, per-person volume, and first-visit tips.
The test calls worked with three of us on three different network.

My biggest mistake here was a wrong diagnosis. When a laptop and phone test failed,
I assumed a networking limit and built a relay server. The real cause was my work
network's firewall blocking the video connections. The relay is still unproven,
but seems redundant at this point.

Working with Claude was fast at building and testing, but I had to correct its facts several times,
including the firewall cause Claude has misdiagnosed and whether test calls were successful.
Overall, this was a tough experience as we were all using unfamiliar tech stacks. Many of our pieces
of the project ended up having dependencies of another persons contributions, so there was a lot
of back and forth that had to occur. The boundaries between pieces weren't clear enough before we
got started.
